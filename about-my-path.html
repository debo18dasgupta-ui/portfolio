01 Amazon Japan
Fraud Investigation

Learning to Investigate Human Behavior

As part of Amazon's Transaction Risk Management team, my job was to determine whether a buyer or seller was engaging in fraudulent activity.

Every case required answering a simple question: what is really happening here?

I analyzed account activity, reviewed patterns, gathered evidence, and made decisions based on incomplete information. Looking back, this wasn't very different from UX research. Both require:

  • Understanding human behavior
  • Identifying patterns
  • Investigating root causes
  • Making informed decisions from limited data
The biggest lesson I learned was that assumptions can be dangerous. Evidence matters.
02 Tech Mahindra
Information Architecture

Understanding Information Architecture at Scale

At Tech Mahindra, I worked on improving the accuracy of map and address data for the Japanese market.

Addresses in Japan are structured very differently from those in many other countries. Ensuring accuracy meant understanding how users search, navigate, and interpret location information.

Information is only useful when it is organized in a way people can understand.

Today, when I think about navigation systems, content hierarchy, or information architecture, I often find myself drawing on the same mindset.

03 Cognizant
Search & User Intent

Understanding Search and User Intent

At Cognizant, I supported Huawei's Petal Search expansion into Japan. My role involved analyzing Japanese-language search results and helping improve search quality and relevance.

This experience introduced me to a concept that sits at the heart of UX: user intent.

People rarely search for exactly what they mean — they search with goals, expectations, assumptions, and context. Understanding what users are trying to accomplish, and helping them get there, became a recurring theme throughout my work.

05 — Reflection

I wasn't changing careers.
I was changing tools.

My path into design wasn't traditional. I didn't start with a design degree — I started with language, data, and problem-solving.

That background continues to influence how I approach design today, combining analytical thinking, cultural understanding, and empathy to create experiences that are both useful and intuitive.
