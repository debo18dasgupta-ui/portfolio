About Me

I taught myself design from scratch — then spent a year learning from people who actually ship.

A year ago, I couldn't tell you the difference between auto-layout and absolute positioning. Today, I've built 40+ documented components, run 8 usability testing sessions, and sat shoulder-to-shoulder with engineers shipping production code.

My internship wasn't about fetching coffee. I prepped design files for handoff, built interactive prototypes for stakeholder reviews, audited screens for accessibility, and learned how to take feedback without taking it personally.

I worked in Japanese-speaking teams — I picked up the language along the way, and while I'm far from native, it taught me to communicate design across language barriers.

Self-taught foundation

Learned design through online courses, tutorials, and a lot of late nights in Figma.

Real-world reps

What I've Learned

Skills picked up
during a year of real work.

Here's what a year of shipping, collaborating, and occasionally messing up taught me. Not claiming mastery — just honest, hands-on experience.

🔍

User Research

Ran usability tests, synthesized interview notes into actionable insights, and helped prepare research decks for stakeholder presentations.

⬜

Wireframing & Prototyping

Created low-fidelity wireframes and interactive prototypes in Figma — from paper sketches to clickable flows for user testing sessions.

◈

Design Systems

Built and documented reusable components with auto-layout and variants. Learned how token-based theming works and why consistency matters at scale.

🎨

Visual & UI Design

Translated wireframes into polished high-fidelity screens. Paid attention to typography, spacing, color, and the details that make an interface feel intentional.

👥

Cross-functional Collaboration

Sat in sprint planning, worked alongside engineers during implementation, and learned how to communicate design decisions to PMs and stakeholders.

⊕

Accessibility Awareness

Audited interfaces for WCAG compliance, flagged contrast and focus-state issues, and learned why designing for everyone isn't optional — it's the baseline.
